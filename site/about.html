MedCoreX is a private study library: one person's preparation, organised with intent and opened to a small set of readers. Every file on the shelf was built the same way — backward from the exam itself, forward into books, drills and papers.

The door

The shelf is open to anyone who looks. The library was built carefully and deliberately, and for now it stays simply that — open. Walk in, read everything, nothing stands between you and the stack.

The standard

The syllabus is the spine. Scope comes from the official syllabus — never from memory, never from whichever textbook happened to be open. If it isn't on the syllabus, it doesn't earn screen time, however interesting it is.

MCQs are the bloodstream. The exam is a hundred multiple-choice questions, so practice runs through everything on the shelf. Exposure is measured in questions attempted, not pages read.

The error log is sacred. Every wrong answer earns a written record: the date, the source, your answer, the correct one, and the discriminator you missed. Reading explanations is not the work. Naming why you missed it is.

What's in it

Seven groups carrying the full collection: complete system books from cardiology to ophthalmology, a merged question bank across six subject groups, every recalled past-paper session with its verified key, the intelligence layer that says what the next paper will test, and quick-reference sheets built for the last night before.

Your data

There are no accounts to hold your data: no sign-up, no stored profiles, no analytics, no third-party scripts, no advertising of any kind. You come, you read, you leave — the house remembers nothing about you.

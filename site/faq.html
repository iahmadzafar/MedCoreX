Questions, answered plainly

Accounts, access, and the small print of belonging to the shelf.

What is MedLib X?

A private study library — 36 files across seven groups: complete system books, a merged question bank, recalled past-paper sessions with verified keys, exam intelligence, and quick-reference sheets. Reading the shelf is open to anyone with a browser; opening files takes an account.

Why do I need an account to read a file?

The library is built carefully and deliberately small. An account is the reading desk: it keeps the place quiet, and it means every reader chose to be here. There's no cost, no trial, no paywall — just a door.

How does signing up work?

Email and a password — one minute, no email verification loops. When the account is created, MedLib X assigns you a username derived from your email, made unique at reserve. You can change it later from your account page.

What does my username mean?

It's your seat at the desk. It shows when you're signed in, next to your account. If the exact name you'd want is busy, a numbered one is assigned instead — and you're welcome to change it from the account page, as long as it's free.

I forgot my password.

The shelves are small and the desk is human: write to the librarian and a reset is arranged by hand. Ask for it from the account you signed up with, so ownership is clear.

Can I change my password or email?

Passwords change any time from your account page — it asks for your current password first, the way a locked room should. Email changes also go through the librarian, since the email is how your seat is identified.

Does MedLib X track me or show ads?

No. The account holds your email, your username and a salted password hash — nothing else. No analytics, no third-party scripts, no advertising. Read the longer version on the About page.

Can I share files or sign in on another device?

Sign in wherever you like — the shelf travels. Files are licensed for the account holder's personal use, so pass the door link on, not the books: send someone the front desk instead.

How current is the material?

Kept current and exact is part of the house standard. Recalled sessions sit beside their verified keys, corrected keys are marked as such, and anything superseded is pulled from the shelf rather than left smelling stale.

Something's broken or a file looks wrong.

Tell the librarian — bugs get fixed before furniture gets replaced. Say which file, which group, and what you saw; the shelf is maintained with patience on purpose.

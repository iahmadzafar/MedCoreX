BELL'S PALSY

Acute onset unilateral LMN facial weakness

Inability to close eye, wrinkle forehead, smile

Hyperacusis (stapedius muscle)

Loss of taste anterior 2/3 tongue

Postauricular pain may precede weakness

Management:

Prednisolone 1 mg/kg × 7-10 days (start within 72 hours)

Eye protection (artificial tears, tape eye at night)

Acyclovir if Ramsay Hunt suspected

Prognosis: 70% complete recovery in 3-6 months

VASCULAR TERRITORIES (Most Important for Vivas)

  • ANTERIOR CIRCULATION (Carotid Territory)
    • Middle Cerebral Artery (MCA) - Most Common
      • Dominant hemisphere (usually left)
        • Contralateral hemiparesis (face and arm > leg)
        • Contralateral hemisensory loss
      • Aphasia
        • Broca (expressive): Non-fluent speech, comprehension intact
        • Wernicke (receptive): Fluent but nonsensical, poor comprehension
        • Homonymous hemianopia
        • Gaze preference (eyes look toward lesion)
      • Non-dominant hemisphere (usually right)
        • Contralateral hemiparesis
        • Neglect (left-sided neglect most common)
        • Anosognosia (unawareness of deficit)
    • Anterior Cerebral Artery (ACA) - Rare
      • Contralateral leg weakness > arm
      • Urinary incontinence
      • Behavioral changes, apathy
  • POSTERIOR CIRCULATION (Vertebrobasilar)
    • Posterior Cerebral Artery (PCA)
      • Homonymous hemianopia (contralateral visual field loss)
      • Visual agnosia
      • Memory impairment (if bilateral - hippocampal)
    • Basilar Artery
      • "Locked-in syndrome" (if bilateral pontine)
      • Quadriplegia, facial paralysis
      • Intact consciousness, can only move eyes vertically
    • Vertebral Artery / PICA (Lateral Medullary - Wallenberg Syndrome)
      • Ipsilateral: Facial pain/temp loss, ataxia, Horner syndrome
      • Contralateral: Body pain/temp loss
      • Dysphagia, dysarthria, nystagmus
      • Vertigo, nausea

STROKE RISK FACTORS

  • Non-modifiable
    • Age (↑ risk after 55)
    • Male gender
    • Family history
    • Prior stroke/TIA
  • Modifiable (Treatment Targets)
    • Hypertension (most important)
    • Diabetes mellitus
    • Atrial fibrillation (cardioembolic)
    • Smoking
    • Hyperlipidemia
    • Obesity
    • Physical inactivity

DIAGNOSIS

  • First test: Non-contrast CT head
  • Differentiates: Ischemic vs hemorrhagic (MUST do before thrombolysis)
  • Ischemic stroke: May be normal in first 24 hours, later shows hypodensity
  • Hemorrhagic stroke: Hyperdense (white) area immediately
  • MRI (more sensitive)
    • DWI (Diffusion-weighted imaging): Shows acute ischemia within minutes
    • More sensitive than CT, but less available in Pakistan
  • Other investigations
    • ECG (atrial fibrillation)
    • Echocardiography (cardiac source of embolus)
    • Carotid Doppler (carotid stenosis)
    • Blood: Glucose, lipids, complete blood count
    • HbA1c, VDRL (if young stroke)

ACUTE MANAGEMENT (TIME IS BRAIN)

HYPERACUTE PHASE (First 4.5 hours)
  • Eligibility for Thrombolysis (tPA - Tissue Plasminogen Activator)
    • Inclusion criteria
      • Clinical diagnosis of ischemic stroke
      • Symptom onset <4.5 hours
      • Age >18 years
      • CT scan shows NO hemorrhage
    • Exclusion criteria (Major)
      • Hemorrhage on CT
      • Recent surgery/trauma (<3 months)
      • Previous intracranial hemorrhage
      • Active bleeding
      • BP >185/110 mmHg (uncontrolled)
      • Glucose <50 or >400 mg/dL
      • Platelets <100,000
      • INR >1.7 (on warfarin)
      • Recent stroke (<3 months)
      • tPA Dose: 0.9 mg/kg (max 90 mg)
      • 10% as bolus
      • 90% as infusion over 1 hour
Complication: Intracranial hemorrhage (6% risk)
  • Post-tPA care
    • Monitor BP closely (keep <180/105)
    • No antiplatelet/anticoagulant for 24 hours
    • Repeat CT if neurological deterioration

GENERAL ACUTE STROKE MANAGEMENT

  • A-B-C-D-E Approach
    • Airway: Protect if ↓ GCS, aspiration risk
    • Breathing: Oxygen if SpO2 <95%
    • Circulation: IV access, pulse oximetry
    • Disability: GCS, pupils — IV fluids (normal saline); correct glucose if <60 or >300 mg/dL
    • Exposure: Full exam; treat fever (paracetamol)
    • Everything else: Monitor BP - permissive hypertension (allow BP up to 220/120) - brain needs perfusion
    • Aspirin 300 mg loading, then 75 mg daily (if NO thrombolysis)
    • If thrombolysis given: Start aspirin after 24 hours
    • DVT prophylaxis (if immobile)
    • Swallow assessment before oral intake
    • Early mobilization, physiotherapy
DO NOT lower BP acutely (except if BP >220/120 or giving thrombolysis)

SECONDARY PREVENTION (Long-term)

  • Antiplatelet therapy
    • Aspirin 75 mg daily + Clopidogrel 75 mg daily × 21 days (dual for 3 weeks)
    • Then clopidogrel alone OR aspirin alone long-term
  • Anticoagulation (if cardioembolic - AF)
    • Warfarin (INR 2-3) OR
    • DOACs (apixaban, rivaroxaban) - preferred if available
  • Risk factor modification
    • Antihypertensives: Target <140/90 (start after acute phase)
    • Statins: Atorvastatin 80 mg (all ischemic strokes)
    • Diabetes control: Target HbA1c <7%
    • Smoking cessation
    • Lifestyle: Diet, exercise, weight loss
  • Carotid endarterectomy
    • If symptomatic carotid stenosis >70%
    • Do within 2 weeks of TIA/minor stroke

INTRACEREBRAL HEMORRHAGE (ICH)

RECOGNITION PATTERN
  • "Sudden severe headache + vomiting + ↓ consciousness"
  • Onset: Sudden
  • Headache: Severe
  • Vomiting: Common
  • Consciousness: Often impaired (unlike ischemic)
  • Focal signs: Present (depend on location)

CAUSES

  • Hypertension (most common - 50-60%)
    • Chronic HTN → lipohyalinosis → Charcot-Bouchard microaneurysms
    • Locations: Basal ganglia (putamen most common), thalamus, pons, cerebellum
  • Other causes
    • Amyloid angiopathy (elderly, lobar hemorrhages)
    • Arteriovenous malformation (AVM)
    • Aneurysm rupture
    • Anticoagulation (warfarin, DOACs)
    • Bleeding disorders
    • Tumors
    • Drugs (cocaine, amphetamines)
    • Trauma

MANAGEMENT

  • Medical
    • BP control: Target SBP <140 mmHg (within first hour) - prevents hematoma expansion
    • Use IV labetalol or nicardipine
  • Reverse anticoagulation if on warfarin/DOACs
    • Warfarin: Vitamin K + PCC (prothrombin complex concentrate)
    • DOACs: Idarucizumab (for dabigatran), andexanet alfa (for Xa inhibitors)
    • Avoid antiplatelet agents
    • Avoid aggressive BP lowering (can worsen outcome)
  • Manage ↑ ICP if present
    • Head elevation 30°
    • Osmotherapy (mannitol, hypertonic saline)
    • Avoid hypotonic fluids
  • Surgical
    • Indicated if
      • Cerebellar hemorrhage >3 cm (risk of brainstem compression)
      • Superficial lobar hemorrhage with neurological deterioration
      • Hydrocephalus (EVD - external ventricular drain)
      • Generally NOT for deep hemorrhages (basal ganglia, thalamus)
    • Prognosis
      • 30-day mortality: 40-50%
      • Predictors: Size, location, GCS, IVH (intraventricular hemorrhage)

SUBARACHNOID HEMORRHAGE (SAH)

RECOGNITION PATTERN
  • "Thunderclap headache" - Sudden, worst headache of life
  • Classic presentation
    • Sudden explosive headache ("worst ever", maximal at onset)
    • Neck stiffness (after few hours)
    • Photophobia
    • Vomiting
    • May have LOC
    • Focal signs if associated ICH

STATUS EPILEPTICUS

Core Concept
  • Seizure lasting >5 minutes OR two or more seizures without return to baseline consciousness
This is a NEUROLOGICAL EMERGENCY - mortality increases with duration
TREATMENT PROTOCOL (Stepwise - Viva Essential)
TIME 0-5 MINUTES: INITIAL STABILIZATION
├─ Airway: Position patient (recovery position)
├─ Breathing: Oxygen 15 L/min
├─ Circulation: IV access, check glucose
├─ If hypoglycemic: IV dextrose 50 mL of 50% immediately
└─ If seizure continues beyond 5 minutes → FIRST LINE
TIME 5-10 MINUTES: FIRST-LINE BENZODIAZEPINES
├─ IV Lorazepam 0.1 mg/kg (max 4 mg) ← PREFERRED if IV access available
├─ OR Rectal Diazepam 0.5 mg/kg (if no IV access)
├─ OR Buccal/IM Midazolam 0.2 mg/kg
└─ If seizure continues → repeat ONCE after 5 min → if still continues → SECOND LINE
TIME 10-20 MINUTES: SECOND-LINE AGENTS
├─ IV Phenytoin 20 mg/kg over 20 minutes

PARKINSON DISEASE

Core Concept
  • Progressive neurodegenerative disorder due to loss of dopaminergic neurons in substantia nigra

CARDINAL FEATURES (4 Features - Need 3 of 4 for diagnosis)

  • Mnemonic: BRAD
  • Bradykinesia - Slowness of movement (ESSENTIAL for diagnosis)
  • Resting tremor - "Pill-rolling" tremor (4-6 Hz), at rest, disappears with movement
  • Axial rigidity - "Cogwheel" or "lead-pipe" rigidity
  • Dyskinesia (Postural instability) - Loss of postural reflexes, tendency to fall

CLINICAL FEATURES (Complete)

  • Motor features
    • Pill-rolling tremor: At rest, hand, 4-6 Hz frequency
    • Bradykinesia: All movements slowed
    • Handwriting becomes small (micrographia)
    • Reduced facial expressions ("masked facies")
    • Reduced arm swing while walking
    • Rigidity: Cogwheel rigidity (resting tremor superimposed on lead-pipe rigidity)
    • Postural instability: Falls backward (retropulsion)
    • Gait: Shuffling, short steps, festination (accelerating), difficulty turning
    • Freezing: Temporary inability to initiate movement
  • Non-motor features (Often preceding motor features)
    • REM sleep behavior disorder (acting out dreams - early feature)
    • Olfactory dysfunction (loss of smell - very early feature)
    • Depression, anxiety
    • Cognitive impairment (late)
    • Autonomic dysfunction (orthostatic hypotension)
    • Constipation
    • Urinary dysfunction
    • Psychosis (usually drug-induced)
